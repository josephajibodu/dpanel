---
title: "Security best practices"
description: "Simple habits that keep your account, servers and data safe."
---

flitops sets secure defaults, but some of security is in your hands. These are the habits that matter most, roughly in order of importance.

## Your account

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Turn on two-factor authentication" icon="shield">
    It's the single most effective thing you can do. A stolen password alone then isn't enough to get in. See [Two-factor authentication](/account/two-factor-authentication). Ask everyone in your team to do the same.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use a unique, generated password" icon="key">
    Let a password manager create and store a long random password for flitops. Never reuse a password from another site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Keep your recovery codes safe" icon="life-ring">
    Store them in your password manager, not in a note on your desktop.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Your team

- **Give the lowest role that works.** Most people who ship code only need **Developer**. Keep **Owner** to one or two people. See [Roles and permissions](/teams/roles-and-permissions).
- **Remove people promptly** when they leave. Their [SSH keys](/connections/ssh-keys) are taken off the team's servers at the same time.
- **Review members now and then.** Remove anyone who no longer needs access, and cancel old invitations.

## Your servers

- **Leave site isolation on** for every site. Isolate older sites with **Isolate this site**. See [Site isolation](/sites/site-isolation).
- **Use SSH keys to isolated sites** for developers, rather than full server access.
- **Never reset a server's identity** unless you know you rebuilt it. An unexpected identity change can mean someone is impersonating your server.
- **Keep packages up to date.** Ubuntu's security updates matter. Log in now and then and run `sudo apt update && sudo apt upgrade`, or schedule it.
- **Don't open database ports to the internet.** Use [server connections](/servers/server-connections) between your servers, and an [SSH tunnel](/servers/connecting-to-a-database) from your computer.
- **Don't add extra firewall rules** at your cloud provider that open more than SSH, HTTP and HTTPS, unless you know you need them.

## Your sites and data

- **Keep secrets in the Environment tab**, never in your Git repository or deploy script.
- **Keep `APP_DEBUG=false`** (or your framework's equivalent) in production. Debug pages can reveal secrets.
- **Keep your dependencies updated.** Most hacked websites are hacked through an outdated plugin or package.
- **Schedule backups**, and keep them in a separate bucket with its own keys. Every backup is verified, but test a restore on a spare database from time to time anyway.
- **Use read-only database users** for reporting tools and anyone who only needs to look at data.
- **Treat downloaded backups as sensitive.** Delete them from your computer when you're done.

## Your provider credentials

- Create **dedicated tokens** for flitops, with only the permissions it needs.
- **Rotate** them if you think they may have been exposed, then reconnect the account in flitops.
- **Revoke** them at your provider if you stop using flitops.

## If you think something is wrong

1. **Change your flitops password** and check two-factor authentication is on.
2. **Remove unknown members** from your teams (their SSH keys come off your servers with them), and check your servers' `authorized_keys` files for keys flitops didn't add.
3. **Rotate** your cloud, Git and storage credentials, and any secrets in your sites' environments.
4. **Check your servers** for unknown users, processes or cron jobs.
5. **Contact us** at [hello@flitops.com](mailto:hello@flitops.com).
