---
title: "Scheduled backups"
description: "Back up a database automatically every hour, day or week, and choose how many backups to keep."
---

A **backup schedule** backs up a database automatically. Each database has its own schedule. You need the **Run backups** permission (Owners, Admins and Developers).

## Setting up a schedule

1. Open the database's **Backups** page (from the server's **Databases** tab, or the site's **Database** tab).
2. In the **Schedule** card, choose:
   - **Storage provider**: the bucket to store backups in. [Connect one](/connections/storage-providers) first if the list is empty.
   - **Frequency**: **Hourly**, **Daily** or **Weekly**.
   - **Keep last**: how many backups to keep (1 to 365). When a new backup completes, older ones beyond this number are deleted from your bucket.
   - **Enabled**: turn the schedule on or off.
3. Save.

The first scheduled backup runs within the next hour. After that, backups run at the chosen interval, counted from the previous scheduled run.

## Choosing a frequency and retention

Think about how much data you could afford to lose, and how far back you might need to go:

| If you… | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Take orders or payments all day | **Hourly**, keep **48** (two days of hourly backups) |
| Run a typical business app | **Daily**, keep **30** (a month) |
| Have a site that rarely changes | **Weekly**, keep **12** (about three months) |

Backups are full copies each time, so keeping more of them uses more storage. Check your storage provider's pricing.

<Tip>If you need both recent and long-term copies, keep a frequent schedule in flitops and add a lifecycle or versioning rule in your bucket to keep older copies for longer.</Tip>

## Backing up now

Click the button to start a backup straight away, for example before a risky migration or deploy. Manual backups appear in the history marked as triggered by you, and count towards the **Keep last** number.

## Pausing a schedule

Turn off **Enabled** and save. Existing backups stay in your bucket. Turn it back on to resume.

## Backup history

The **History** list shows each backup's **Status**, who or what **triggered** it (the schedule, or a person), its **Size**, whether it's **Verified**, and when it was **Created**.

Statuses you'll see while a backup runs include running, verifying, completed and failed.
